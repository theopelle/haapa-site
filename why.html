Why Haapa, and not another app?

Hevy, Strong, Lyfta, and FitNotes are popular, and they do their job. Haapa is for people who want the whole app for free, a plan they can see weeks ahead, and a gym app that feels calm. Here is how they compare.

Checked on 14 November 2026 from each app's store page and help pages. Apps change, so check before you choose. Spotted a mistake? Write to hello@haapa.app and we'll fix it.

Freemium means free to start, with the full app behind a subscription. Four routines and three months of history run out fast if you train on a plan: a Push, Pull, Legs, Upper, and Lower split already needs five routines, and one training block can take three months.

Why people choose Haapa

Everything you need to train on a plan, for free.

  • The whole app, for free. Unlimited routines, exercises, history, and timers. No Pro version, no ads, and no trial that runs out.
  • A plan you can see. The progression grid shows your whole block, exercises in rows and weeks in columns, and your own rule fills in the weeks ahead.
  • HIIT and interval timers built in, so circuits, Tabata, and stretches don't need a second app.
  • No account and no network. Nothing to sign up for, and your workouts never leave your phone.
  • Share a plan without an account. A QR code or a file is enough, for a coach, a personal trainer, or a friend.
  • Easy to switch. Bring your history from Strong, Hevy, or a spreadsheet.

The feel

Calm, warm, and on your side.

Gym apps tend to look clinical white or loud and neon. Haapa takes its colours from a Finnish sauna and a lake at sunset: pale aspen wood, lake blue, and a low ember sun.

Nothing shouts and nothing nags. There are no streaks to protect and no badges to chase. Miss a target and Haapa doesn't scold you: repeat the week, or go on. It's your progress and your choice. When you finish a plan, Haapa shows you what changed, without confetti.

To be fair

What Haapa doesn't do (yet).

  • No watch app
  • No exercise pictures or videos
  • No sync between phones (a backup file does this job)
  • Kilograms only for now, no pounds
  • No friends, feed, or likes

Haapa is also new, so there are no store reviews yet, and it's in English and Finnish only for now.

Which one is for you?

Pick the app that fits.

Haapa

Choose it if you follow your own plan week by week, use interval timers, and want a calm app that keeps your data on your phone, with nothing to pay for later. Coming from Strong or Hevy? Bring your history with you.

Hevy

Choose it if you like training with friends and a social feed, or want a watch app, and you're happy to pay once you outgrow 4 routines.

Strong

Choose it if you want a proven, simple logger and are happy to pay for PRO once you need more than 3 routines.

Lyfta

Choose it if you want a video for every exercise and ready-made programs. Its free version is generous.

FitNotes

Choose it if you're on Android and want a simple, free log that has been around for years.
